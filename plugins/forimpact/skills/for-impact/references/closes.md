# The 15 Closes

A close is a question that keeps the ask a dialogue. Ask until you get one of three outcomes: the commitment, specific information about lower capacity or relationship, or a road map to the commitment.
A close is not a trick or a script. Use your own words. Mix closes; you may use four or five in one visit. After you ask, be quiet and let the prospect respond.
If the first response is not "yes," it is not necessarily "no." Never make the decision for the funder. Present the opportunity and let them decide.

### 1. The Clueless Close

- **What it is:** Put your funding plan (for example, a pyramid of investment levels) in front of the prospect and ask one question: "Where do you see yourself?"
- **Use it when:** You genuinely do not know what to ask for and want to start the dialogue. Do not rely on it when you need the top level; nobody picks the highest number on the sheet.
- **Sounds like:** "Based on everything we've talked about today, where do you see yourself?"

### 2. The Listening Close

- **What it is:** Selling is not telling. Ask an open question, then stop talking and listen. If the prospect unloads with criticism, treat it as passion and take notes. People sometimes talk themselves into funding the work.
- **Use it when:** You visit someone close to the organization, such as a board member, who has never been asked what they think.
- **Sounds like:** "What do you know about [project]?" / "What do you think about the organization?"

### 3. The Qualifying Close

- **What it is:** Put a specific number on the table for a later conversation. Acknowledge that it is early, then ask if they would explore that level. It is non-threatening, but the number is on the table.
- **Use it when:** It feels too soon to ask, and you are tempted to "cultivate" instead. Always ask.
- **Sounds like:** "I want to acknowledge we're just starting to work together. Would it be possible to have a conversation later this year about being one of [number] investors at [amount]?"

### 4. The Math Close

- **What it is:** Also called "the last piece of the puzzle." Break a big goal down step by step: set aside a later phase, subtract what is committed, lay out the lead and the levels. Keep slicing until one piece is left — the prospect's.
- **Use it when:** The goal is big, the numbers are big, and the prospect may ask, "What difference does my investment make?"
- **Sounds like:** "Does this all make sense? Because I'd like to talk to you about helping us with this piece of the puzzle."

### 5. The Binary Close

- **What it is:** Binary, not analog. The question is in or out, not the level. Get the person in now, then build the relationship and the level over time.
- **Use it when:** Their name would lend credibility to the cause, but it is an early visit, with no history and no rationale to point to a high number.
- **Sounds like:** "This one's not even about the number. We just need you in." / "Are you in?"

### 6. The Permission Close

- **What it is:** After you walk through the purpose and the priorities, ask permission to walk through the funding plan and how they could help. If you do not get a "no," you have permission to proceed to the ask.
- **Use it when:** Any visit, at the start or after the purpose. It is most useful when the prospect was not predisposed to talk about money.
- **Sounds like:** "Would it be okay if I walked you through our funding plan and talked about how you could help?"

### 7. The Goonies Close

- **What it is:** "Up there, it's their time. Down here, it's our time." Stop apologizing for the past or for being small. Greatness is a decision, not a process. This gets the prospect to take you seriously.
- **Use it when:** The prospect has heard big promises before with no clear plan, or a board member asks if the organization will ever get there.
- **Sounds like:** "Who cares where we've been? It's our time to do something great." / "This is a serious conversation, and I want you to be excited."

### 8. The Top of the Mountain Close

- **What it is:** The close is what you show, not what you say. Physically take the prospect to the place with the view, show what is and what could be, and ask for help. Then be silent.
- **Use it when:** It is not about the money. The prospect has the capacity and needs to see the vision.
- **Sounds like:** "Here's where we are. Here's the opportunity in front of us, and we need your help." / "Can you help make that happen?"

### 9. The Predisposition Close

- **What it is:** A trusted, visionary person shows the prospect the vision, then hands off. They say someone will come to sit down with the prospect soon. That second person makes the ask.
- **Use it when:** You have a charismatic leader, executive director or visionary board member who can set up the ask for someone else to close.
- **Sounds like:** "In the next few days, [name] will sit down with you. I have no idea what they will ask you for, but I know you'll do whatever you can to help."

### 10. The Action Forcing Event

- **What it is:** An event that forces action: a groundbreaking, a high-level meeting, or a set window such as a number of visits in a number of days. It gives you a reason to go back and ask for a decision.
- **Use it when:** You have many "pendings." Without an action forcing event, a pending commitment can stay pending forever.
- **Sounds like:** "The reason I'm coming to you now is that we need a decision by [date]." / "I meet our lead investor in two weeks, and I'd like to share that we have [number] more commitments."

### 11. The Practice Close

- **What it is:** Make practice visits with real prospects: board members, current and past funders, community members. "Practice" lowers the pressure on you, but you still present the opportunity, and people still commit real money.
- **Use it when:** You are stuck on finding the perfect words. The magic is in having the conversation, not in being perfect.
- **Sounds like:** "I want to practice this with you. Can I walk you through our plan and get your input?"

### 12. The Handful of People Close

- **What it is:** When the prospect says the number is higher than they were thinking, explain that only a handful of people can be asked at this level, and they are one of them. Your funding plan makes this authentic: if you cannot push with your best prospect, there is nobody better to push with.
- **Use it when:** The prospect is one of your few top prospects. Back down only when you learn they do not have the capacity or are not that interested.
- **Sounds like:** "I understand. There are only a handful of people we can talk with at this level, and you're one of them. With that in mind, is it a possibility for you to look at that number?" / "Tell me about that."

### 13. The Contingency Close

- **What it is:** Named for a contingency on a house. It removes an objection: name the concern, assume it is solved, and ask if they would be in.
- **Use it when:** The prospect raises a specific concern that stands between them and a yes.
- **Sounds like:** "Assuming we took care of [concern], would you then be in?"

### 14. The Momentum Close

- **What it is:** When the prospect offers less than you asked, book the smaller amount as a "momentum commitment" and ask permission to keep the larger number in the conversation. It is a relationship, not a transaction.
- **Use it when:** The prospect commits at a lower level, but the larger level is still possible.
- **Sounds like:** "That's fantastic. Would it be okay if we booked the [smaller amount] as a momentum commitment, and continued the dialogue about the [larger amount] level?"

### 15. The Transformational Close

- **What it is:** When one investor's capacity is far above everyone else's, you cannot rationalize their number as a share of the campaign. Frame it as transformation instead: a level of investment that would change how the organization does its work and raise the bar for its impact.
- **Use it when:** You are with someone who could invest far more than anyone else in your funding plan.
- **Sounds like:** "Would it be possible for you to consider a transformational investment? It would completely transform the way we [deliver impact for the cause]."

## More closes

The full "20 Closes" audio is at https://forimpact.org/article/closes/. It adds four more:

- **Higher Level:** When the visit gets lost in details, take it back up to the higher purpose.
- **Holy Audacity:** Name your boldness up front so you have permission to put big numbers on the table.
- **Self-Fulfilling Prophecy:** Put a big number out there and ask the prospect to think about it over time.
- **Commensurate:** Ask for a commitment commensurate with the person's capacity and relationship to the cause.
