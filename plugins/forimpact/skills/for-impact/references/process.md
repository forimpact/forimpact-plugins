# Process: predisposition, the visit, the ask, follow-up

The Process box of the diagnostic. The sales process has three equal parts:

1. **Predisposition:** everything that prevents a cold call.
2. **The visit:** a direct conversation that shares the impact and presents the
   opportunity.
3. **Follow-up:** professional written follow-up within 24 hours.

To prepare a specific visit, hand off to `visit-prep`.

## Predisposition

To make someone inclined, in advance, toward a specific action. The goal is to get
the visit, not to sell on the phone.

**When you are stuck, change the message, the messenger or the medium.** Three kinds
of predisposition assets:

1. **People:** who can open the door? Natural partners, board members, current
   investors.
2. **Places and experiences:** site visits, events, field trips, virtual briefings.
3. **Things:** a wow package, an engagement tool, a video, an impact report.

Approaches:

- A predisposition email or letter that sets up the phone call.
- Have someone else coordinate the visit. An executive's assistant is very
  effective.
- Send something visual, not just an email.
- Never make a cold call. Predispose first.

The three responses, and what to do:

1. **Yes:** confirm the date and time, and send any materials.
2. **Maybe:** offer flexibility, and suggest another way in or another person.
3. **No:** send a thank-you note and move on.

What makes it work:

- **Authenticity.** You cannot mess up a contact if you are authentic.
- **Attitude.** Believe the prospect wants to meet you. It is self-fulfilling.
- **Perseverance.** Do whatever it takes to get the visit. Persistence with variety
  in message, messenger and medium pays off.

## The visit

### Set the intent

State the flow at the start, so everyone knows where the conversation is going:

> "Thanks for taking time to visit with us today. What we'd like to do is take a few
> minutes to get to know you. We'd like to share a little about what we're doing.
> And then, if it's okay, we'd love to talk about ways you could help."

Public: forimpact.org/set-intent-start-visit/

### The flow: Open, Story, Present the Opportunity

1. **The Open.** Build the human connection. Do not talk about the organization yet.
   It takes as long as it takes: twenty seconds or twenty minutes. In fundraising
   mode many people turn into pitch machines; resist it. Look for unexpected
   connections.
2. **The Story.** Share the impact, starting at 30,000 feet. Use questions to pass
   the ball. Use the prospect's words. Let their questions take you down in
   altitude.
3. **Present the Opportunity.** Show the specific ways they can help: Champion,
   Invite, Invest. Use the engagement tool. Be ready with specific amounts. The goal
   is to reach "How can I help?"

Navigating:

- One person leads the visit and manages the flow, tempo, altitude and transitions.
- Use alignment questions and transitions: "With the time we have left, could we
  talk about what a funding partnership might look like?"
- Redirect rabbit holes back to the flow.

### One clear goal for each visit

Set one functional goal before every visit:

1. **Qualify:** is this the right prospect (capacity, relationship, timing)?
2. **Predispose:** set up the next engagement or the ask.
3. **Roadmap:** get a clear path to the commitment.
4. **Ask:** present the opportunity for investment.

### Dialogue, not pitch

- A pitch is a linear, scripted monologue. It works on a competition stage, not
  one-to-one.
- A dialogue adapts. It takes in the prospect's words and concerns.
- Research backs this: a 2025 study in *Nature* found persuasive effectiveness rose
  81% in an interactive format, and brains synchronize in real dialogue.
  (forimpact.org/funding-pitch-vs-dialogue-the-science-says-dont-pitch/)
- Get the funder talking. Ask questions to pass the ball.

## The ask

### Just ask

- This is most of what you need to know about raising money. Until you ask, a
  prospect cannot say yes.
- About 90% of generosity is unlocked by asking.
- Success is proportional to the number of times you ask face-to-face, or have a
  dialogue about how someone can help. If you asked three times last quarter, how do
  you get to six?
- Just Visit. Just Ask.

Just Ask does NOT require:

- Perfect timing. Timing will never be perfect.
- Knowing exactly what to ask for.
- Several prior visits.
- Full board alignment.
- No nerves. Feeling sick before an ask is normal.
- Perfect messaging or materials.

Public: forimpact.org/article/just-ask-just-ask-just-ask/

### Champion, Invite, Invest

A structured answer to "How can I help?":

1. **Champion** the cause and the case: speak for it, lend your voice.
2. **Invite** others to get involved and engaged: introductions to people the
   organization should meet.
3. **Invest** with a commensurate commitment, to help fund the vision.

Each yes builds momentum toward the investment.

### Ask until

Keep the ask a dialogue until you reach one of three outcomes:

1. **The commitment.**
2. **Specific information** about lower capacity or the relationship.
3. **A road map** to the commitment.

### A proper ask

- The specific help you want.
- The specific project, program or level.
- A clear dollar figure.
- Example: "We need your help. Would you consider underwriting this project at
  $20,000?"

For close techniques, see `closes.md`.

## Today, Tomorrow, Forever

The relationship-based funding model organizes funding around impact and the
relationship, not siloed campaigns.

| Timeframe | Gives from | Looks like |
|---|---|---|
| **Today** | Cash flow | Annual investment; giving circles with set levels (for example, $1K or $10K) |
| **Tomorrow** | Assets | Multi-year and major commitments |
| **Forever** | Estate | Legacy and planned commitments, endowment |

**The Triple Ask** presents all three in one visit. It shows the full funding
strategy, matches how people actually give, lets investors protect their
investment through a legacy commitment, and maximizes the relationship.

Public: forimpact.org/the-for-impact-relationship-based-funding-model/

## Follow-up

### The 24-hour rule

Complete all follow-up within 24 hours of the visit: with your organization, with
the prospect and with yourself. 80% done at 24 hours beats 90% done in three weeks.

### Engagement has a half-life

Engagement fades fast after a visit. Its half-life is about 24 hours. Fast
follow-up captures it at its peak.

### Principles

- Most commitments close in follow-up.
- Follow-up is a strategy, not an action item.
- The ball is always in your court.
- Professional written follow-up is a must.

### Write it with the prospect's words

During the visit, capture:

- Attributable quotes: what they said that you can reference.
- Points of connection.
- Specific interests and concerns.
- The next steps you agreed.

When you can, draft the follow-up in or right after the visit, while their words
are fresh.

### Keep control of the next step

- "Could I check in with you next Friday?"
- "Can we talk after your visit with your financial planner?"
- "Would it be okay if I followed up in two weeks?"

### Memo for the record

After every visit, write down:

- **Discovery:** what you learned about them.
- **Flow of the visit:** notes on presenting the opportunity.
- **Attributable quotes.**
- **Action and follow-up required:** clear next steps.
