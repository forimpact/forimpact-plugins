# Canonical For Impact prep checklists

These are your completeness checks when assembling a visit brief. They are
NEVER shown to the user as forms to fill out — the conversation fills them, and the
brief renders what's known plus named homework for what isn't.

## Knowledge Base Worksheet (per prospect)

- **CAPACITY** — the rating and where it came from (referrals, business, screenings,
  grants to peer organizations, other). Evidence, not vibes: "board member says they
  could underwrite the whole campaign"; "they fund similar orgs at $500K–$1M."
- **RELATIONSHIP** — to the organization, case, CAUSE, project. A good name needs a
  relationship to the cause (not to you) plus capacity; whether you can get in the
  door affects prioritization and strategy, never whether they're a good name.
  Include connectors and natural partners. Note who HOLDS the relationship
  (founder-held? board-held?).
- **GIFT HISTORY** — keep it simple: last, largest, total. Don't forget planned gifts.
- **TIMING** — around you/the organization (order of visits, best moment), more than
  around the prospect's life.
- **OTHER GIFTS** — known philanthropy elsewhere: organizations, sizes, existing
  pledges.
- **ADDITIONAL DISCOVERY/RESEARCH REQUIRED** — the named homework.
- **NOTES** — for corporations/foundations: the right officers, primary contact,
  internal champion.

## Prospect Strategy Checklist (the master strategy for one prospect)

- **PROSPECT**
- **RELATIONSHIP MANAGER** (the project manager for this name — one owner)
- **DECISION MAKER**
- **NATURAL PARTNER / CHAMPION**
- **KNOWLEDGE BASE** — see worksheet above
- **RELATIONSHIP GOALS** — what would maximizing this relationship mean? (Maximized =
  in the last 18 months: visited 1:1, engaged at altitude, AND had a how-can-you-help
  conversation. All three or it isn't maximized.)
- **HOOKS / SUGGESTED PROJECTS / AREAS OF INTEREST**
- **ADDITIONAL RESEARCH REQUIRED**
- **POSSIBLE CHALLENGES**
- **PREDISPOSITION IDEAS** — assets (people, places/experiences, things); if stuck,
  turn one of three dials: message, messenger, medium
- **PRESENTATION DESIGN / THE "EXPERIENCE"** — engagement pathway: how can they
  experience, taste, and see the impact?

## Visit Checklist (before every visit — a tool, not a form)

- **WHAT do we want to accomplish?** (Goals — ONE functional goal: qualify /
  predispose / road map / ask. Stepping stones, not stages: be prepared on any visit
  for "what do you need?")
- **WHAT do we need to learn on the visit?** (Discovery)
- **VISIT MECHANICS** — WHERE is the best place? WHEN is the best time? WHO needs to
  be there? (Presentation team, one visit leader, roles set before the visit)
- **HOW will we predispose for this visit?** (Strategy and actions: open the door +
  set the table — impact, team, purpose of the discussion. The number is never a
  surprise in the room.)
- **DECLARE the three A's:**
  - **ATTITUDE** — the energy you bring; never make a decision for the prospect
  - **ALTITUDE** — people engage you at the altitude of your choosing; hit the
    altitudes (engage at 30,000', navigate to where the money goes)
  - **ASK** — if all else fails, the one thing you can ask that hits your goals
- **FLOW of the visit:**
  - Script the opening/agenda (permission-based: "would that be all right?")
  - Script discovery questions and transitions toward goals
  - Potential challenges/objections and responses
  - Script the ASK (scenarios): the ask(s); the answer to "how can I help?"
    (Champion / Invite / Invest at minimum); the answer to "I can't"; a secondary or
    qualifying ask (e.g. the capacity-surfacing move: "I have no idea what your
    capacity is — where do you see yourself?")
- **ENGAGEMENT TOOL(S) FOR THE VISIT** — the visual, shoulder-to-shoulder, number
  positioned so the funder can self-select up or down
- **FOLLOW-UP PLAN** — within 24 hours (engagement has a 24-hour half-life); follow-up
  is predisposition in reverse; when possible, draft it with the prospect in the room;
  capture attributable quotes verbatim

## Ask-until and the close library (for scripting the ask)

Continue the ask dialogue until one of three outcomes: a commitment, specific
information (capacity/relationship), or a road map to the commitment.

Frequently-used closes for the brief's script section:

- **Clueless close** (first visit / unknown capacity): "I have no idea what your
  capacity is. Where do you see yourself?" — with levels in hand.
- **Predisposition close** (short virtual visits; "too early for numbers"): "So that
  we might come back and make a considered request in [month], I wonder if it might
  be all right to walk through our funding plan and get your input."
- **Road map** (stalled institutional relationships): "For our planning and yours —
  what would a year-one partnership look like? What funding level are you thinking,
  so we can plan on our end?"
- **Action-forcing event** (asks pending into oblivion): "We're trying to leverage all
  of our commitments for [date/event]. Would it be possible to have a commitment by
  [date] so we can leverage it one way or another?"
- **Permission bridge** (when "how can I help?" doesn't come): "With your permission,
  I'd love to talk about our funding plan and ways we might work together."
